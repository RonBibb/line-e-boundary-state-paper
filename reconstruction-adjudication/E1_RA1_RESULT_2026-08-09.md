## 7. Effect on Paper E1

Paper E1 remains viable with a sharper central question:

> Which parts of a singular boundary state are reconstructible from reduced
> asymptotic field data, and which require independent global metric data?

The anchor ablation supplies a valuable negative result rather than a hidden
defect. It demonstrates that agreement of normalized invariants does not fix
absolute boundary scale.

The manuscript may claim:

- predictive, non-endpoint-copy reconstruction for the tested smooth

- closure under the explicit future-normal parity convention; and
- no classification change under the two constraint normalizations.

It may not claim that the four field functions completely determine the full
boundary state or its absolute clock normalization.

## 8. Effect on the greater E/M program

The test identifies a concrete version of the framework's previously noted
absolute-scale deficit. A future Line-M scattering or selection law must map
not only normalized anisotropy and matter data but also the intrinsic/global

Symbolically, a physical map must act on

\[
(\mathfrak B_{\rm shape},c_\lambda)_-
\longmapsto
(\mathfrak B_{\rm shape},c_\lambda)_+,

normalized shape variables is underdetermined with respect to absolute scale.
