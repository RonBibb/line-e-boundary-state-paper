\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:detune}
Keep $a,b$ and the speed in Theorem~\ref{th:fourier} fixed.
For every sufficiently large resonant $k$ there is a
homogeneous background close to the equal-weight background
at which $T_k=0$. Its ratio of weights is $A/B=1+\delta_k$,
where
\[
 k^2\delta_k\longrightarrow
   \frac{u^2+v^2}{6}+\frac{uv}{24},\qquad
   u=\frac1a,\quad v=\frac1b.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $S_\pm$ for $S_{\pm1/4}(b;k)$.
The perturbed multiplier vanishes exactly at
$\delta_k=-(S_++S_-)/S_+$, since the first resonant
coefficient of $S_+$ is nonzero. The ratio of the
$k^{-3}$ term in the sum to the $k^{-1}$ term of
$S_+$ gives the limit by \eqref{coefficient}.
The center of the chosen semicircle can vary
$A/B=\sinh r_a\sinh r_b$ through one with nonzero
derivative. These weights are therefore attained
by nearby backgrounds.
\end{proof}

This accumulation of exact mode cancellations prevents a
uniform inverse for the partial-record differential in a
neighborhood of the equal-weight background. At the example
times above, the limiting coefficient is $0.39701362$.
The value of $k^2\delta_k$ at $k=16384$ is $0.39701361$.
