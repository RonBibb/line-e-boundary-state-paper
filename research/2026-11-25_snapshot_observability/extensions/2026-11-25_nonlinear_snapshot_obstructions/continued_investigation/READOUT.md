# Gowdy investigation readout

**Lead with the geometry of what two slices reveal.** Two vacuum Gowdy histories can have identical induced metrics on both observed slices. At other placements, the spatial metric itself supplies missing velocity information. In an explicit circular-loop class, two full metric slices recover both velocities stably through an open range of observation times, including irrational gaps at which the orbit fields alone have arbitrarily weak modes.

## Recommended paper

**First paper.** *What Two Metric Slices Determine in Vacuum Gowdy Spacetimes*. Start with a pair of exact histories that match both measured metrics. Then show how the metric momentum constraint makes both missing velocities observable on a short open interval, even at irrational gaps where the orbit fields fail. The general criterion from the two null-ray transports explains both results. Keep the recovery of asymptotic fields as one scoped corollary, and use the archived reconstruction as a concise motivating application. The circular-loop examples are locally homogeneous and must be described with their marking. The asymmetric two-mode construction supplies the stronger boundary-isometry statement.

**Pitch.** Two spatial geometries can fail to determine a vacuum Gowdy history. Yet the same kind of geometric data can stabilize the inverse under a criterion that reads the evolving spatial curve along successive null rays. The momentum constraint is the source of the extra information, and an explicit two-slice family shows the difference in the same equation at the same times.

## Proposed abstract for the focused vacuum paper

We study which velocity information is determined by two induced metrics on marked areal slices in vacuum Gowdy spacetimes. We construct distinct histories with identical measured metrics on both slices, including a pair with the same limiting speed and different limiting metric potentials. For the linearized two-slice problem, the two characteristic transports determine the orbit-field response, and the momentum constraint supplies a further projection through the metric gradient. We give rational and irrational criteria for stability at high frequency. In a circular-loop class we prove local Lipschitz recovery of both initial velocities on an open interval of delays, and exhibit longer placements with arbitrarily weak modes even for the full metrics. In a separate open subcritical regime, a stable finite observation map determines the asymptotic fields with Lipschitz dependence.
