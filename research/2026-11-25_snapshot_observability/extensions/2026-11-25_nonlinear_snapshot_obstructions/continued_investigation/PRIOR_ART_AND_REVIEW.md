1. Check the sign convention in the metric, the periodicity of the Ernst transform, and the allowed torus-equivariant boundary maps in the two-mode example. Check that the equality claims specify the slices and components and that the asymptotic comparisons carry the same marking.
2. Check the one-smoothing-order remainder in the two null-ray formula. For the two-component unknown, verify the essential-spectrum obstruction at irrational gaps and the exact rational holonomy criterion. For the rank-two version with explicit placements, verify the orthogonal projection away from the free two-time range and the noncollinearity of the sideband coefficients.
3. For the terminal metric gradient, verify the transported projection from the momentum constraint, the dark finite-ray packets for necessity, and the backward estimate from radial terminal data. Verify the explicit short-delay constants and the composition with the low-velocity tail.
4. Check the lower-bound regimes of the mixed Jacobi channels and the nonlinear circular-loop detuning. The three-derivative loss is a normalized linearized statement; the cubic inverse Hölder obstruction applies to the specified signed pairs.
5. Check the metric-modulus endpoint dual sequence and the Hardy estimate in the nonlinear quadratic trace. Distinguish the earlier finite metric anchor from any unsupported recovery with no anchor.

## Follow-up theorem

The two-metric high-frequency tests leave a possible finite-dimensional kernel on general inhomogeneous backgrounds. The circular-loop reverse estimate removes it on an explicit open interval. A focused follow-up is to test whether that last step follows from a local condition on a general spatial orbit curve, or whether some admissible curves actually have a finite-mode kernel even with both metrics supplied. The finite-mode obstruction and a high-frequency weak mode would give different answers.
