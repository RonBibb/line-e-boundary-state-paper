# Gowdy claim-to-literature and citation audit
## 25 November 2026

**The closest omitted antecedent is a numerical paper in the very journal that received the manuscript.** Amorim, Bernardi and LeFloch tested two-direction Gowdy evolution, documented failure with steep data, explicitly described starting with ordinary Cauchy data and reconstructing backwards, and proposed changing the unknowns at exceptional points. The present work implements one finite-window restart and quantifies its error. A second omitted paper gives the correctly normalized true-spike formula used here and a parameter-matching procedure. Together they materially reduce the novelty assessment. [Amorim et al. §§3, 4.2.3–4.2.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0811.1266) · [Lim et al. Appendix A, especially (A3)–(A6)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0904.1546)

### Discovery, recency and access

Searches used *singular initial value problem*, *asymptotic velocity*, *AVTD/AVD*, *homeomorphism between regular and asymptotic data*, *final data*, *forward-backward* and *backward-forward evolution*, *spiky velocity*, *matching exact solutions*, *Dirac observables*, *velocity-dominated observables*, and *singularity scattering maps*. Backward checks included the detailed Beyer–LeFloch preprint’s reference [1], the spike transformation lineage in Lim, and the numerical and rigorous sources underlying Li’s summary theorem. Targeted forward searches in INSPIRE used cited-record queries for Amorim et al. (801914), Lim (762823), Rendall–Weaver (554625) and Niedermaier–Sedighi Jafari (3115064). They returned 6, 40, 79 and 2 indexed citing records respectively on this search. Counts document search scope only. Primary texts, rather than index descriptions, ground the main matrix. The 2026 observables paper was public before the August submission and reached its published form in October. A November 10 preprint by the same authors extends the post-Carrollian observable construction. It addresses ultralocal observables in velocity-dominated Carroll gravity and an expansion for Einstein gravity. Its abstract does not claim a predictive finite-window Gowdy spike reconstruction. This later preprint helps delimit the current field and could not have informed the August editorial decision. [Published Gowdy paper](https://doi.org/10.3842/SIGMA.2026.097) · [November preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14644)

I inspected the indicated full-text passages for the directly competing numerical protocols, exact formulas, spike theorems, geometric data and recent Gowdy observable result. Metadata and abstracts were sufficient for incidental analogies and the November extension. The Lim 2008 publisher endpoint did not yield a usable PDF; the author preprint and the later primary publication resolve the convention issue relevant here. INSPIRE coverage and terminology searches are incomplete by construction. I found no identical publication of these particular holdout/ablation numbers. Absence of an identical table does not confer conceptual priority or demonstrate significance.
